\begin{abstract}
Multimodal neurobiological prediction can combine complementary information from imaging, genetics, clinical measurements, biospecimens, cognition, and behavior, but real cohorts contain heterogeneous subject-specific missingness. Existing methods commonly aggregate only the observed subset or reconstruct unavailable modalities, while paying less attention to how generated information should be distinguished and weighted during the final decision. We propose TRACE, a framework based on \emph{Token Reconstruction and an Availability-aware Confidence-weighted Ensemble}. TRACE reconstructs each missing modality in a shared token space by cross-attending to the same subject's observed modalities and tags every token as observed or generated. A Transformer with sparse top-$k$ experts models cross-modal interactions, after which joint, unimodal, and pairwise classifiers produce complementary predictions. Their outputs are fused using explicit availability masks, modality reliability, and branch-level predictive evidence; the reported branch probabilities and weights therefore reproduce the final prediction exactly. We evaluate TRACE on a family-disjoint ABCD-ADHD cohort with eight modalities and an ADNI three-class task with four modalities. TRACE obtains the strongest mean result across all reported ADNI metrics, including $64.88\%$ accuracy, $63.75\%$ macro-F1, and $67.37\%$ macro-AUPRC. On the imbalanced ABCD-ADHD endpoint, TRACE achieves the highest accuracy and specificity and remains competitive in AUROC and AUPRC, although no AUPRC comparison establishes significant superiority after multiple-comparison correction. These results show that subject-conditioned reconstruction and reliability-aware decision fusion can support heterogeneous incomplete inputs without obscuring the source of the final probability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Neurobiological phenotypes are characterized through heterogeneous sources that describe different levels of the same subject, including brain structure and function, genetic susceptibility, cognitive and clinical measurements, and behavioral or environmental factors~\cite{ref_jack2018niaaa,ref_lane2018ad,ref_scheltens2021lancet}. These sources are not interchangeable: each may provide diagnostic information that is absent from the others, while their agreement can strengthen a prediction~\cite{ref_zhang2011multimodal,ref_suk2014deep,ref_venugopalan2021}. Multimodal learning therefore offers a natural basis for neurodevelopmental outcome prediction and neurodegenerative disease stratification. Its practical value, however, depends on whether a model can use the evidence that is actually available for each subject rather than assuming a fixed, complete input set.

This assumption is frequently violated in research cohorts and clinical data. Modalities can be absent because of acquisition cost, protocol differences, contraindications, participant dropout, or failed quality control, producing many subject-specific availability patterns~\cite{ref_mlmm_survey,ref_wu2024mlmm,ref_hemis2016,ref_ma2021smil,ref_m3care}. Restricting analysis to complete cases discards otherwise informative subjects, whereas replacing every missing modality with a population-level constant removes the dependence between the unavailable source and the subject's observed evidence. Robust incomplete-modality learning must consequently address two coupled questions: how to infer a task-relevant representation for an unavailable modality, and how strongly that inferred representation should influence the final prediction. Existing aggregation and alignment methods avoid explicit completion but may leave recoverable cross-modal information unused~\cite{wang2023shaspec,lin2023missmodal}. Generative approaches estimate missing observations or features, but many are specialized to commensurate imaging modalities or optimized primarily for reconstruction fidelity~\cite{ref_wu2018mvae,ref_sutter2021gmelbo,li2025agdic,zhou2026acadiff}. Flex-MoE supports arbitrary modality combinations through a missing-modality bank and sparse routing~\cite{ref_flexmoe}, yet its bank is indexed by the availability pattern and is therefore shared by subjects with the same observed subset. Less attention has been paid to jointly making completion subject-conditioned and making the downstream use of generated evidence reliability-aware.

Missingness also changes what constitutes a useful explanation. A sparse router indicates where computation is allocated, but its gate value is not, by itself, the contribution of a modality to the prediction. Likewise, post-hoc attribution can audit a trained model, yet it need not reproduce the decision rule used during inference~\cite{ref_lundberg2017shap,ref_sundararajan2017ig,ref_ribeiro2016lime,ref_rudin2019stop}. For an incomplete subject, an explanation must additionally distinguish an unavailable branch from an active but uncertain one, and it should not assign equal trust to directly observed and generated inputs. This motivates a decision mechanism in which availability, source reliability, predictive confidence, and branch contribution are represented explicitly and used in the prediction itself.

We propose \emph{TRACE: Token Reconstruction and an Availability-aware Confidence-weighted Ensemble}, a unified framework for missingness-aware multimodal classification. TRACE first maps every available modality to a common token space. For each unavailable target modality, a target-specific generator uses learned queries to cross-attend to the subject's observed tokens, producing a conditional latent reconstruction; source embeddings retain whether each token was observed or generated. A Transformer with sparse top-$k$ experts then models interactions among the resulting modality tokens. Rather than relying on a single opaque fusion head, TRACE forms joint, unimodal, and pairwise diagnostic branches and combines their probability outputs according to branch availability, modality reliability, predictive evidence, and a low-capacity branch prior. The reported branch predictions and weights are therefore the same quantities used to construct the final prediction, providing a direct prediction-level decomposition rather than a separate post-hoc explanation. Training further exposes the model to reduced-modality views and constrains their predictions against the corresponding more complete views.

We evaluate TRACE on two naturally incomplete cohorts: the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) Study and the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI)~\cite{garavan2018abcd,ref_adni}. The results reveal different but informative operating regimes: TRACE attains the strongest mean performance across the reported ADNI metrics, while remaining competitive on the imbalanced ABCD-ADHD endpoint, where its gains are concentrated in accuracy and specificity and do not establish statistically significant AUPRC superiority. This distinction avoids reducing incomplete-modality performance to a single favorable metric and motivates the following contributions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate incomplete multimodal prediction as a coupled reconstruction-and-decision problem and introduce a unified framework that preserves the distinction between observed and generated evidence throughout the prediction path.
    \item We develop subject-conditioned latent reconstruction in which each missing modality is generated from the available evidence of the same subject and supervised by leave-one-modality-out targets from complete training subjects.
    \item We introduce a missingness-aware reliability--evidence fusion mechanism over joint, unimodal, and pairwise branches. Its active branches, predictions, and normalized weights directly compose the final probability and can therefore be inspected without fitting an auxiliary explainer.
    \item We study the framework on naturally incomplete ABCD and ADNI data with heterogeneous modality types, using multi-metric evaluation and multiplicity-corrected confirmatory analysis for the imbalanced ABCD endpoint rather than selecting a single favorable metric.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_works}

\subsection{Representation Learning with Missing Modalities}

Incomplete multimodal learning aims to exploit complementary sources without requiring every modality for every sample~\cite{ref_mlmm_survey,ref_wu2024mlmm}. Reconstruction-free methods operate directly on the observed subset. HeMIS aggregates available latent features through permutation-invariant statistics~\cite{ref_hemis2016}; SMIL applies Bayesian meta-learning to severe missingness~\cite{ref_ma2021smil}; and M3Care models relations between patients and modalities in incomplete healthcare records~\cite{ref_m3care}. Other methods constrain incomplete and complete views to occupy compatible representation spaces. ShaSpec separates shared and modality-specific factors and regularizes their distributions~\cite{wang2023shaspec}, whereas MissModal combines geometric, distributional, and semantic constraints across availability conditions~\cite{lin2023missmodal}. These approaches avoid the cost and possible artifacts of synthesis, but their aligned or pooled representation does not explicitly state what information is supplied for a particular missing modality.

Generative methods instead recover absent observations or latent features. Multimodal variational autoencoders combine modality-specific posteriors to infer a shared latent variable~\cite{ref_wu2018mvae}, and generalized multimodal evidence lower bounds extend training to multiple observed subsets~\cite{ref_sutter2021gmelbo}. In incomplete neuroimaging, AGDiC couples adaptive graph learning, normalizing flows, and masked cross-attention to recover latent modality features under arbitrary missingness~\cite{li2025agdic}. ACADiff uses clinically conditioned latent diffusion to synthesize missing MRI and PET volumes from the available images and metadata~\cite{zhou2026acadiff}. Such methods are well suited to modalities with meaningful cross-modal generation targets, but observation-level reconstruction can be unnecessarily expensive when the downstream task uses heterogeneous vector-valued sources. TRACE therefore reconstructs target-modality tokens rather than raw observations and supervises the generator on held-out representations from complete training subjects, focusing completion on a shared space used by the classifier.

Between these two families, pattern-conditioned completion offers a lighter alternative. Flex-MoE learns a missing-modality bank indexed by the observed-modality combination before applying sparse expert fusion~\cite{ref_flexmoe}. This design supports arbitrary combinations efficiently, but every subject with the same availability pattern initially receives the same bank representation. TRACE instead conditions each target-modality reconstruction on the current subject's observed tokens. The generated tokens are also source-tagged, allowing downstream reliability estimation to distinguish them from direct observations.

\subsection{Sparse Experts and Missingness-Aware Fusion}

Conventional fusion applies essentially the same transformation to every sample even when the available modalities and their relevance differ. Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) introduces conditional computation by routing each token to a small subset of expert networks. V-MoE demonstrated this principle at scale in vision Transformers~\cite{riquelme2021vmoe}, while MoE++ augments learned feed-forward experts with zero, copy, and constant experts to allocate both computation and transformations heterogeneously~\cite{jin2025moepp}. For incomplete multimodal learning, Flex-MoE combines a generalized router trained with complete samples and specialized routing for observed-modality combinations~\cite{ref_flexmoe}. These methods establish sparse routing as a useful mechanism for representation specialization under heterogeneous inputs.

A related line of work structures experts around the type of cross-modal information being modeled. MMoE separates experts for unique, redundant, and synergistic information~\cite{yu2024mmoe}. I$^2$MoE adds weakly supervised interaction objectives and sample-specific reweighting to quantify these interaction types locally and globally~\cite{xin2025i2moe}. This interaction-aware view is more informative than undifferentiated concatenation, but it addresses a different question from evidence reliability. Router or interaction weights determine how a representation is computed; they do not necessarily measure how much the resulting prediction should be trusted, especially when a modality is generated or absent. TRACE therefore uses sparse experts for feature transformation and a separate reliability--evidence mechanism for combining diagnostic branches.

\subsection{Interpretability and Decision Decomposition}

Clinical and neurobiological models are often audited with post-hoc feature attribution. SHAP, integrated gradients, and LIME estimate the sensitivity or local contribution of inputs under different reference, perturbation, or surrogate constructions~\cite{ref_lundberg2017shap,ref_sundararajan2017ig,ref_ribeiro2016lime}. Multimodal variants shift the unit of analysis from individual features to information sources: MM-SHAP quantifies modality contributions and can expose unimodal collapse~\cite{parcalabescu2023mmshap}, while Layer-Wise Modality Decomposition traces modality-specific contributions through a pretrained fusion network~\cite{park2025lmd}. These methods are valuable for auditing arbitrary architectures, but the resulting attribution is external to the original prediction rule and may change with the explanation protocol.

Interaction-based interpretation instead asks whether modalities contribute unique, redundant, or synergistic information. MMoE and I$^2$MoE encode these categories in specialized experts~\cite{yu2024mmoe,xin2025i2moe}, and LSMI estimates sample-level interactions using pointwise information-theoretic quantities~\cite{yang2025lsmi}. Such analyses characterize relationships among modalities, but they do not automatically decompose the probability produced by a classifier. This distinction becomes important under missingness: a pairwise interaction may be unavailable, and a branch supported by generated features may warrant less trust than the same branch supported by observations. TRACE addresses this decision-level requirement by explicitly predicting with joint, unimodal, and pairwise branches, masking unusable branches, and normalizing their contributions using modality reliability and predictive evidence. The resulting decomposition is faithful to the fusion rule in the limited but concrete sense that the reported branch probabilities and weights exactly reconstruct the model's final probability; it is not presented as a causal explanation of the underlying disease process.


\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}
\label{sec:method_overview}

The overall framework of TRACE is illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:TRACE}. Each observed modality is first encoded as a sequence of tokens in a shared latent space, while the subject-specific availability pattern is recorded. TRACE then performs conditional latent completion to reconstruct each missing modality from the observed modalities of the same subject and attaches a source tag that distinguishes generated representations from direct observations (Section~\ref{sec:reconstruction}). Next, the observed and reconstructed tokens are jointly processed by a sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) Transformer to model cross-modal interactions and produce task-aligned modality features (Section~\ref{sec:sparse_moe}). Finally, a reliability-aware decision module forms unimodal, pairwise, and joint predictions and combines them according to input reliability, predictive confidence, and a learned branch prior (Section~\ref{sec:evidence_fusion}). The resulting branch probabilities and weights directly compose the final class probability.

Let \(\mathcal{M}=\{1,\ldots,M\}\) denote the modality set. For subject \(n\), \(\mathbf{x}_n^m\) is the input of modality \(m\), \(o_n^m\in\{0,1\}\) indicates whether the entire modality is observed, and \(y_n\in\{1,\ldots,C\}\) is the class label. A modality-specific encoder maps each heterogeneous input to \(P\) tokens in a shared \(D\)-dimensional space:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{Z}_n^m=E_m(\mathbf{x}_n^m),\qquad
\mathbf{Z}_n^m\in\mathbb{R}^{P\times D}.
\label{eq:tokenization}
\end{equation}
The observed-modality mask is retained separately from values missing within an otherwise available modality. Figure~\ref{fig:TRACE} follows the complete computation in four stages: input availability, conditional completion, sparse MoE fusion, and decision decomposition.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Figures/TRACE.pdf}
  \caption{Overview of TRACE. The availability mask identifies observed and missing modalities. Each missing token sequence is reconstructed from the same subject's observed modalities and tagged as generated. Shared self-attention exchanges cross-modal information, after which top-\(k\) routing selects a sparse set of experts and recombines their outputs into modality features. Unimodal, pairwise, and joint branches then produce separate probabilities. Their activity, reliability, confidence, and learned priors determine inspectable branch weights, and each weighted branch probability contributes directly to the final prediction.}
  \label{fig:TRACE}

\subsection{Conditional Completion}
\label{sec:reconstruction}

A population-level replacement cannot adapt to the observed evidence of an individual subject. TRACE instead assigns each target modality \(m\) a conditional generator \(G_m\). For the observed set \(\mathcal{O}_n=\{j:o_n^j=1\}\), the target-specific context is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}_n^m=
\operatorname{Concat}\!\left(
\{\mathbf{Z}_n^j:j\in\mathcal{O}_n\setminus\{m\}\}
\right).
\label{eq:context}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}_n^m
=G_m(\mathbf{Q}_m,\mathbf{C}_n^m),
\qquad \mathbf{Q}_m\in\mathbb{R}^{P\times D},
\label{eq:generator}
\end{equation}
where learned target queries pass through \(L\) cross-attention blocks and a lightweight output gate. The \(P\) query positions provide capacity for later token-level cross-modal interaction; we do not assume a one-to-one semantic correspondence between generated and observed token positions.

The reported configuration trains the generators by leave-one-modality-out completion on complete training subjects. For a held-out observed target \(m\), the remaining modalities form the context and the projected mean representation is matched with a stop-gradient target using cosine distance:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}
=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|}
\sum_{(n,m)\in\mathcal{R}}
\left[
1-\cos\!\left(
R_m(\overline{\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}}_n^m),
\operatorname{sg}[R_m(\overline{\mathbf{Z}}_n^m)]
\right)
\right],
\label{eq:reconstruction_loss}
\end{equation}
where the overline denotes token averaging and \(\mathcal{R}\) is the sampled set of eligible subject--target pairs. Supervision is intentionally applied at the pooled subject-representation level rather than asserting token-wise semantic alignment. At inference, a missing target is generated from the available non-empty context. Classification gradients are stopped through generated tokens in the reported configuration, so \(G_m\) is optimized through Eq.~\eqref{eq:reconstruction_loss} rather than the diagnosis label.

Completion alone does not communicate uncertainty to the predictor. Observed and generated tokens therefore receive different learned source embeddings:
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}_n^m=
\begin{cases}
\mathbf{Z}_n^m+\mathbf{e}^{\mathrm{obs}}, & o_n^m=1,\\
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}_n^m+\mathbf{e}^{\mathrm{gen}}, & o_n^m=0.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:source_embedding}
\end{equation}
The two source embeddings are shared across modalities. They restore a common token layout while preserving whether each modality representation is observed or generated.

\subsection{Sparse MoE Fusion}
\label{sec:sparse_moe}

Different subjects now have the same token layout but different mixtures of observed and generated content. TRACE concatenates all source-tagged tokens and uses global self-attention for cross-modal exchange. The dense feed-forward block is replaced by a sparse MoE layer:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{MoE}(\mathbf{h})
=\sum_{e\in\operatorname{TopK}(\mathbf{g})}
\pi_e(\mathbf{h})F_e(\mathbf{h}),
\qquad
\pi_e=
\frac{\exp(g_e)}
{\sum_{r\in\operatorname{TopK}(\mathbf{g})}\exp(g_r)},
\label{eq:sparse_moe}
\end{equation}
where \(F_e\) is expert \(e\), \(\mathbf{g}\) is a noisy content-dependent router, and only the top-\(k\) experts are evaluated. During training, the observed-modality combination index provides auxiliary supervision that encourages missingness-sensitive routing; the inference route remains determined by token content. A load-balancing loss discourages persistent expert collapse. The output tokens \(\mathbf{H}_n^m\) are reduced to a modality feature \(\mathbf{f}_n^m=\operatorname{Pool}_m(\mathbf{H}_n^m)\) by a stable mean summary with a learned attentive residual. This pooling detail is not treated as a separate contribution.

\subsection{Decision Decomposition}
\label{sec:evidence_fusion}

Let \(u_n^m=1\) when modality \(m\) is directly observed or can be reconstructed from at least one observed source, and \(u_n^m=0\) otherwise. The usability mask protects branch construction when generation is disabled or no conditioning source exists; it is distinct from provenance. TRACE estimates modality reliability as
\begin{equation}
r_n^m=
\begin{cases}
1, & o_n^m=1,\\
\sigma\!\left(s_m^{r}(\mathbf{f}_n^m)+b_m^{g}\right),
 & m\text{ is generated},\\
0, & m\text{ is unusable},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:modality_reliability}
\end{equation}
where \(b_m^g\) is initialized negatively. Thus the reported model anchors observed modalities at unit reliability and learns subject-specific trust only for generated representations.

The model constructs three complementary prediction scales: unimodal branches represent modality-specific evidence, pairwise branches model low-order cross-modal complementarity, and the joint branch captures global interaction. Let \(\mathcal{B}\) contain one joint branch, \(M\) unimodal branches, and all \(\binom{M}{2}\) pairwise branches. Their logits are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\boldsymbol{\ell}_n^{0}
 &=F_0([\mathbf{f}_n^1;\ldots;\mathbf{f}_n^M]),\\
\boldsymbol{\ell}_n^{m}
 &=F_m(\mathbf{f}_n^m),\\
\boldsymbol{\ell}_n^{ij}
 &=F_{ij}([\mathbf{f}_n^i;\mathbf{f}_n^j;
 \mathbf{f}_n^i\odot\mathbf{f}_n^j;
 |\mathbf{f}_n^i-\mathbf{f}_n^j|]).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:branch_logits}
\end{equation}
Element-wise products and absolute differences are computed only after modality encoders and the shared Transformer have mapped all modalities to a task-aligned common dimension; they are interaction features, not claims of coordinate-wise biological correspondence. A branch is active only when its required modalities are usable. Its provenance-conditioned quality is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
q_n^{0}
 &=\frac{\sum_m u_n^m r_n^m}
 {\sum_m u_n^m},\\
q_n^{m}&=r_n^m,\qquad
q_n^{ij}=\sqrt{r_n^i r_n^j}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:branch_quality}
\end{equation}

Each branch also supplies a prediction-dependent confidence proxy:
\begin{equation}
e_n^b=\sum_{c=1}^{C}\operatorname{softplus}
(\ell_{n,c}^b),\qquad
\kappa_n^b=\frac{e_n^b}{e_n^b+C}.
\label{eq:evidence_confidence}
\end{equation}
It is used only as a relative weighting signal and is not interpreted as calibrated correctness. Combining activity \(A_n^b\), quality, confidence, and a learned branch prior \(\beta_b\) gives the normalized weight directly:
\begin{equation}
w_n^b=
\frac{A_n^b(q_n^b+\epsilon)(\kappa_n^b+\epsilon)\exp(\beta_b)}
{\sum_{r\in\mathcal{B}}A_n^r(q_n^r+\epsilon)(\kappa_n^r+\epsilon)\exp(\beta_r)}.
\label{eq:branch_weight}
\end{equation}
The final class probability is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{p}_n
=\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}}w_n^b
\operatorname{softmax}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_n^b),
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\ell}_n=\log(\mathbf{p}_n).
\label{eq:final_prediction}
\end{equation}
The stored branch probabilities and weights therefore reconstruct the model output exactly. This is a faithful decomposition of the fusion rule, not a causal explanation of disease mechanisms.

For exploratory visualization, branch mass can be allocated to modalities by assigning each unimodal weight to its source, splitting each pairwise weight equally, and distributing the joint weight uniformly:
\begin{equation}
d_n^m=w_n^m+
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\ne m}w_n^{(m,j)}
+\frac{w_n^0}{M}.
\label{eq:modality_weight}
\end{equation}
We call \(d_n^m\) \emph{modality-level decision allocation}. Unlike Eq.~\eqref{eq:final_prediction}, this branch-to-modality split is a visualization heuristic and is not a mathematically unique attribution. Token attention may be shown within a modality for inspection, but it is likewise not presented as causal importance.

\subsection{Training Objective}
\label{sec:objective}

TRACE is trained on an original view and an artificially reduced view that independently drops observed modalities while retaining at least one source. To expose the hierarchy of the objective, we group its terms into task supervision, completion, MoE balancing, and missingness robustness:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}
 &=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}+\lambda_b\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{branch}},\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bal}}
 &=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gate}}^{+}
 +\tfrac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gate}}^{-},\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rob}}
 &=\lambda_d\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{drop}}
 +\lambda_s\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sd}}
 +\lambda_m\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MoFe}},\\
\mathcal{L}
 &=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{task}}
 +\lambda_r\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}}
 +\lambda_g\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{bal}}
 +\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rob}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:total_objective}
\end{equation}
Here \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cls}}\) supervises the fused prediction, whereas \(\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{branch}}\) averages cross-entropy over active branches and weights samples within each branch by detached branch quality. This prevents weak generated evidence from dominating auxiliary supervision.

Let \(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{+}\) and \(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{-}\) denote the original- and reduced-view logits. The reduced-view classification and self-distillation terms are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{drop}}
 &=\operatorname{CE}(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{-},y),\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{sd}}
 &=T^2\operatorname{KL}\!\left(
 \operatorname{sg}[\mathbf{p}_{T}^{+}]
 \,\|\,\mathbf{p}_{T}^{-}\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:view_losses}
\end{equation}
The more-versus-fewer (MoFe) consistency term penalizes the original view only when its per-sample classification loss exceeds that of its strict reduced subset:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MoFe}}
=\left[
\operatorname{CE}(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{+},y)
-\operatorname{CE}(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{-},y)
\right]_{+}.
\label{eq:mofe}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:total_objective} retains MoE specialization while keeping the primary learning target explicit: reliable diagnosis under naturally incomplete and artificially reduced modality sets.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}

We evaluate TRACE on two naturally incomplete multimodal cohorts. Subjects are retained when at least one selected modality is available; complete-case filtering is not applied.

\noindent\textbf{ABCD-ADHD.}
The Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) Study is a large-scale longitudinal study of adolescent brain, cognitive, behavioral, and health development~\cite{garavan2018abcd}. We use baseline parent K-SADS full ADHD present-or-past as a binary research endpoint and assessed available-field-negative participants as controls. This endpoint supports algorithmic evaluation and is not presented as a clinical diagnosis. The family-disjoint cohort contains \(11{,}671\) participants, divided into \(8{,}325\) training, \(1{,}679\) validation, and \(1{,}667\) test participants. The corresponding positive counts are \(1{,}145\), \(228\), and \(245\). Eight baseline modalities are included: structural MRI, resting-state fMRI, diffusion MRI, genetic ancestry/population structure, neurocognition, physical health, non-target mental health, and environment.

\noindent\textbf{ADNI.}
The Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) is a multicenter longitudinal study supporting biomarker-based research on cognitive decline and Alzheimer's disease~\cite{ref_adni}. We use the three-level diagnosis code supplied in the prepared cohort as the classification target. Four modalities are included: structural MRI-derived Image features, Genomic features, Clinical assessments, and Biospecimen measurements (IGCB). The cohort contains \(2{,}116\) participants, with \(1{,}480\), \(318\), and \(318\) assigned to the training, validation, and test splits, respectively. The exact semantic mapping of the three diagnosis codes must be confirmed against the frozen ADNI data dictionary before submission.

\subsection{Preprocessing and Leakage Control}
\label{sec:preprocessing}

All feature selection, imputation, scaling, model selection, and threshold selection use training or validation data only. Test data are accessed after configurations are frozen. Whole-modality absence is recorded by a binary mask and remains distinct from missing entries inside an otherwise available modality.

For ABCD, each modality table is aligned to the immutable participant split. Numeric features exceeding the prespecified training-set missingness threshold or falling below the variance threshold are removed. Remaining entries are imputed with training-set medians and standardized using training-set statistics. Target-defining K-SADS ADHD fields, target-revealing source tables, direct ADHD proxies, and aggregate features that could reconstruct those proxies are excluded. Genetic-family members are assigned to only one split, although sites may occur across partitions; the experiment is therefore family-disjoint but not site-held-out.

For ADNI imaging, the latest FreeSurfer record per participant is retained according to its update timestamp. Structural features beginning with \texttt{ST} and ending in regional volume, thickness, or surface-related suffixes are selected, mean-imputed from training participants, and standardized with training statistics. Genotypes are filled using the training-set modal genotype and min--max transformed to \([-1,1]\) using training-only extrema. Clinical fields associated with diagnosis timing are excluded, and missing Clinical and Biospecimen values are filled with training-set column means. An unavailable modality remains absent after within-modality preprocessing and is reconstructed only inside TRACE.

\subsection{Baselines, Training, and Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

We compare TRACE with six incomplete-multimodal baselines. Flex-MoE uses an official-code-derived adaptation of its missing-modality bank and sparse routing~\cite{ref_flexmoe}. I$^2$MoE uses an official-code adapter for unique, redundant, and synergistic interaction experts~\cite{xin2025i2moe}. MoE++-corrected is implemented through the I$^2$MoE adapter with learned, zero, copy, and constant experts~\cite{jin2025moepp}. AnyMod is reimplemented from its modality-query and task-anchor formulation~\cite{feng2024anymod}. AGDiC-inspired and ACADiff-inspired are task-matched adaptations rather than direct reproductions of the original neuroimaging generation systems~\cite{li2025agdic,zhou2026acadiff}. These qualifiers are retained in the tables because differences in input representation and released code prevent the inspired methods from being interpreted as exact reproductions.

All methods are run for \(50\) epochs with seeds \(0\), \(1\), and \(2\); tables report the test mean and population standard deviation. TRACE uses \(P=16\) tokens per modality, hidden dimension \(D=128\), one fusion layer, four attention heads, \(16\) sparse experts, and top-\(4\) routing. Each target-modality generator contains two cross-attention layers with four heads. Optimization uses AdamW with learning rate \(10^{-4}\), a five-epoch warm-up, gradient clipping at \(5\), modality-dropout probability \(0.25\), and loss weights \(\lambda_g=0.01\), \(\lambda_r=1.0\), \(\lambda_b=0.1\), \(\lambda_d=0.1\), \(\lambda_s=0.15\), and \(\lambda_m=0.1\). ABCD uses batch size \(64\), dropout \(0.3\), zero weight decay, sampler power \(0.35\), and class-weight power \(0.15\). ADNI uses batch size \(32\), dropout \(0.3\), and weight decay \(0.01\). Experiments are executed on a single NVIDIA RTX 5070 Ti GPU.

For ABCD-ADHD, imbalance-handling profiles are selected by three-seed validation AUPRC and frozen before formal test evaluation. Checkpoints maximize validation positive-class AUPRC, and the binary threshold maximizes validation macro-F1. Because the \(14.70\%\) test prevalence makes accuracy insufficient, ADHD-AUPRC is the primary metric; balanced accuracy, macro-F1, weighted-F1, ADHD-F1, sensitivity, specificity, AUROC, and Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) are also reported. Confirmatory comparisons use \(10{,}000\) whole-family paired randomizations, \(10{,}000\) family/seed bootstrap replicates, and Holm correction across the six TRACE-to-baseline comparisons. For ADNI, checkpoints maximize validation macro-F1, and evaluation uses accuracy, balanced accuracy, macro-F1, weighted-F1, macro-AUROC, and macro-AUPRC.

\subsection{Component and Decision Analyses}
\label{sec:pending_analyses}

The main tables evaluate the complete framework, but they do not isolate which components produce the observed behavior. The frozen revision will therefore include controlled ablations of (i) subject-conditioned reconstruction versus no completion and pattern-conditioned static fill; (ii) sparse experts versus a parameter-matched dense feed-forward layer; (iii) reliability--evidence fusion versus uniform branch averaging; (iv) removal of the reliability or evidence factor individually; and (v) removal of reduced-view training. Each variant must reuse the same splits, validation protocol, seeds, and task-specific metrics as the main comparison. Numerical values are intentionally omitted until these reruns are complete.

The final analysis will also stratify performance by the number and identity of naturally available modalities. This is necessary because a global mean can hide failure on rare missingness patterns. For the decision decomposition, we will report observed-versus-generated reliability, joint/unimodal/pairwise branch mass, and modality-level decision allocation \(d_n^m\) by class and missingness pattern. Faithfulness of the probability decomposition itself does not require a surrogate test: Eq.~\eqref{eq:final_prediction} reconstructs the model output exactly. Clinical or biological validity, however, requires separate expert review and stability analysis and will not be inferred from visually plausible weights alone.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The two cohorts expose different aspects of incomplete multimodal learning. ADNI contains four broad modality families and yields a consistent best-mean profile for TRACE across classification and ranking metrics. ABCD contains eight modalities and a substantially imbalanced binary endpoint. There, TRACE favors specificity and overall accuracy, while MoE++-corrected achieves better sensitivity-balanced and positive-class metrics. The cross-cohort result therefore supports the framework's ability to operate under heterogeneous modality sets, but also shows that reliability-aware fusion does not remove the need to select operating points and objectives that match the clinical or scientific cost of errors.

TRACE combines three established mechanisms---conditional reconstruction, sparse experts, and weighted prediction branches---in a single missingness-aware pipeline. The contribution is the way information status is preserved across them: reconstruction is subject-conditioned, generated tokens are explicitly tagged and assigned learned reliability, and the final probability remains decomposable into active branch predictions. This is an architectural and decision-level contribution rather than a new theory of multimodal information decomposition. In particular, a large branch weight means that the branch had high relative quality and evidence under the learned fusion rule; it does not prove that the corresponding modality caused the outcome or represents a clinically valid biomarker.

Several limitations remain. First, the released generators are trained with leave-one-modality-out targets from complete subjects but may receive a smaller partial context at inference. This creates a context-distribution shift that should be tested against pattern-aware reconstruction training. Second, classification gradients are stopped through generated tokens. This limits label leakage into reconstruction but may also prevent task-specific adaptation of the generator; the tradeoff requires ablation. Third, the evidence-derived confidence in Eq.~\eqref{eq:evidence_confidence} is a relative weighting signal rather than a calibrated correctness probability. Calibration error, reliability diagrams, and sensitivity to common logit shifts should be evaluated before confidence terminology is interpreted clinically.

The experimental design also has dataset-level limitations. ABCD is family-disjoint but not site-held-out, and its ADHD label is an algorithmic research endpoint rather than a prospective diagnosis. ADNI uses a prepared three-level code whose semantic mapping and release version must be frozen explicitly before submission. Natural missingness can itself encode acquisition or cohort processes, so performance does not establish robustness to interventions that change which modalities are measured. Finally, several baselines are adapters or inspired implementations rather than exact reproductions, external-cohort validation is absent, and the component, calibration, and missingness-stratified analyses remain to be completed.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented TRACE, a framework for incomplete multimodal learning that combines subject-conditioned token reconstruction, sparse-expert interaction modeling, and an availability-aware confidence-weighted branch ensemble. TRACE distinguishes observed from generated information throughout the prediction path and expresses the final probability as a normalized mixture of joint, unimodal, and pairwise predictions. On ADNI, TRACE achieves the strongest mean across the reported metrics; on ABCD-ADHD, it is competitive but exhibits a specificity--sensitivity tradeoff and does not significantly improve the primary AUPRC endpoint. These results support further study of reliability-aware decision fusion while underscoring the need for frozen ablations, calibration, missingness-stratified evaluation, and external validation.
